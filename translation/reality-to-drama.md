# Reality-to-Drama Translation

## Purpose

Convert lived, researched or imagined reality into selective dramatic action without flattening complexity into exposition or reproducing reality without shape.

## Core claim

Drama is not reality copied. It is reality selected, compressed and arranged around pressure, choice and consequence while preserving the human mechanism that makes the behaviour credible.

## Translation sequence

1. **Source reality** — identify the event, condition, relationship, memory, culture or emotion being translated.
2. **Human mechanism** — determine what participants perceive, believe, want, fear and misunderstand.
3. **Dramatic question** — define what uncertain outcome the scene makes active.
4. **Pressure** — introduce the force that makes avoidance increasingly costly.
5. **Choice** — locate the behaviour that reveals priorities under pressure.
6. **Consequence** — change knowledge, power, intimacy, danger, commitment or possibility.
7. **Expression** — select action, speech, image, sound, object, space and rhythm that carry the mechanism.
8. **Omission** — remove explanation already conveyed through behaviour or form.
9. **Audience path** — sequence information so the audience predicts, revises and feels rather than merely receives.
10. **Production test** — verify that the intended effect survives actual staging and editorial conditions.

## Selection rules

Select details that do at least two jobs: reveal character, establish pressure, carry culture, alter interpretation, create performance opportunity, support motif or enable consequence. Reject details that merely decorate familiarity.

## Compression rules

Compression may combine time, people, locations or events, but must not erase the causal truth that gives the material meaning. When compression changes responsibility, cultural context or moral interpretation, label it as an adaptation choice.

## Failure modes

- documentary accumulation without dramatic selection;
- invented conflict unrelated to the source mechanism;
- emotional explanation replacing behaviour;
- symbolic imagery imposed before repetition earns meaning;
- composite characters that erase necessary differences;
- chronology preserved at the expense of causality;
- spectacle that changes tone without changing the dramatic landscape;
- reducing a culture or profession to recognizable shorthand;
- making every beat maximally clear and eliminating participation by the audience.

## Diagnostic questions

- What specific reality is being preserved?
- What has been altered, and why?
- Which human mechanism makes the scene inevitable but not predictable?
- What changes because this scene occurs now?
- Could the same beat belong to an unrelated project?
- What does the audience infer before it is confirmed?
- What can be removed without losing causal or emotional truth?

## Repair order

1. Restore the source mechanism.
2. Clarify the active dramatic question.
3. Increase pressure through consequence, not noise.
4. Give the decisive behaviour to the character with the greatest stake.
5. replace explanation with observable choice.
6. remove transferable details.
7. retest audience comprehension and production feasibility.

## Acceptance tests

Approve only when the scene preserves meaningful truth, contains a causal turn, creates a specific audience experience, cannot be transferred unchanged to another project, and can be executed without relying on explanatory prose.
