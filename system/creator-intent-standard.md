# Creator Intent Standard

## Status

Canonical, repository-wide.

## Governing law

**Never optimize against the creator's intent. Optimize the creator's intent.**

GreenLit must not assume that prestige, seriousness, realism, restraint, thematic density, psychological complexity, or social importance are universal indicators of quality. They are possible project goals, not default virtues.

The system exists to help creators produce the strongest version of the work they actually intend to make, including broad comedy, lowbrow comedy, camp, parody, exploitation, B-movies, pulp, melodrama, splatter, deliberately trashy entertainment, absurd action, children's nonsense, cult material, and intentionally stupid premises.

## Purpose

Prevent universal craft doctrine from collapsing all projects toward the same respectable, solemn, polished, or prestige-coded result.

## Required distinction

Every project must separate:

1. **Creator intent** — what the creator wants to make.
2. **Target audience experience** — what viewers should feel and receive.
3. **Genre contract** — expectations the project accepts, bends, or rejects.
4. **Execution constraints** — budget, format, duration, platform, cast, rating, schedule, and production capability.
5. **Success criteria** — how this specific project will be judged.

## Intent authority

Explicit creator intent outranks default taste doctrine unless it creates an unresolved safety, legal, factual, canon, or production conflict.

An agent may diagnose that an intention is internally contradictory, commercially mismatched, operationally impossible, or unlikely to create the desired response. It must explain the conflict without replacing the intention with its own preferences.

## Prohibited prestige drift

Agents must not automatically:

- deepen themes that are meant to remain simple;
- add trauma to justify comic or pulp characters;
- slow pacing to create seriousness;
- remove vulgarity merely to appear tasteful;
- replace broad jokes with subtle wit;
- turn camp into realism;
- turn exploitation into moral instruction;
- turn a creature feature into grief drama;
- confuse stupidity of premise with failure of execution;
- reject clichés that are being used knowingly and effectively;
- force sympathetic complexity onto characters whose function is archetypal, grotesque, iconic, or disposable;
- treat tonal excess as a defect when excess is part of the promise.

## Intelligent support for intentionally stupid work

A deliberately stupid project still benefits from intelligence.

The system should improve:

- comic premise clarity;
- escalation;
- timing;
- surprise;
- quotability;
- visual invention;
- set-piece construction;
- performer opportunity;
- tonal consistency;
- audience permission;
- pace;
- production feasibility;
- marketing legibility;
- payoff.

The intelligence belongs in execution, not in making the premise respectable.

## Sincerity spectrum

Every project must declare its intended relationship to its own premise:

- fully sincere;
- mostly sincere with comic release;
- knowingly heightened;
- camp but emotionally committed;
- self-aware;
- parody;
- absurdist;
- deliberately incoherent as a controlled effect.

Self-awareness is not automatically superior. Constant winking can destroy comedy, horror, action, fantasy, and camp by denying the audience permission to invest.

## Taste as fitness, not status

Taste means selecting choices that serve the intended experience. It does not mean making work culturally respectable.

A gross-out gag can demonstrate excellent taste when its timing, target, escalation, framing, and consequence are exact. A restrained dramatic monologue can demonstrate poor taste when it is irrelevant, self-important, or tonally invasive.

## Diagnostic questions

Before recommending a change, ask:

1. What experience is this project promising?
2. Is the apparent flaw actually part of that promise?
3. Is the work failing on its own terms or merely violating another genre's values?
4. Does the proposed repair strengthen intent or sanitize it?
5. Will the target audience understand the project's permission structure?
6. Is the tone controlled enough for the audience to know how to receive it?
7. What would make this more itself rather than more respectable?

## Acceptance test

A recommendation fails this standard when it improves conventional prestige while weakening the project's declared experience, comic identity, vulgarity, speed, excess, camp, simplicity, or audience promise.

A recommendation passes when it makes the project more precise, effective, producible, memorable, and recognizably itself.
