# Creative Council Standard

## Purpose

High-value creative work should pass through multiple bounded perspectives while presenting the creator with one coherent answer.

The Knowledge Engine uses one orchestrator, several specialist reasoning passes, and one final synthesis. It must never expose a noisy swarm of competing agents as the product experience.

## Default council

The orchestrator selects only the passes relevant to the task:

1. **Intent Custodian** — verifies the Intent Contract and anti-goals.
2. **Canon Editor** — identifies authoritative facts, conflicts, open questions, and protected elements.
3. **Story Architect** — evaluates causality, structure, escalation, setup, payoff, and long-horizon consequences.
4. **Character Psychologist** — tests appraisal, motive, protection strategy, choice, contradiction, and belief change.
5. **Audience Simulator** — maps attention, prediction, comprehension, emotion, memory, and intended surprise.
6. **Scene or Artifact Specialist** — performs the actual screenplay, bible, pitch, production, or packaging work.
7. **Human Writing Editor** — removes generic phrasing, over-explanation, false profundity, uniform rhythm, and model residue.
8. **Producer Pass** — protects value-to-cost, actor opportunity, visual clarity, feasibility, and package usefulness.
9. **Continuity Editor** — identifies downstream changes, stale documents, contradictions, and synchronization requirements.
10. **Adversarial Critic** — searches for the strongest credible reason the artifact may fail.

## Pass contract

Each pass returns only:

- findings;
- proposed changes;
- evidence or rationale;
- confidence;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- whether the issue is blocking or advisory.

A pass may not independently rewrite the whole artifact unless assigned as the artifact specialist.

## Synthesis rules

The orchestrator must:

- resolve disagreements according to creator intent, canon authority, and weighted success criteria;
- preserve useful contradictions rather than smoothing every edge;
- prefer targeted repair over total regeneration;
- distinguish objective defects from taste choices;
- include only changes that materially improve the declared result;
- deliver one unified artifact, not stitched-together committee prose.

## Escalation levels

### Light

For small, local, low-risk changes:

- Intent Custodian
- Artifact Specialist
- Human Writing Editor
- applicable acceptance gates

### Standard

For normal scene, character, or document work:

- Intent Custodian
- Canon Editor
- Story or Character pass
- Audience Simulator
- Artifact Specialist
- Human Writing Editor
- Continuity Editor

### Deep

For architecture, mythology, endings, full drafts, or expensive production decisions:

- all relevant council passes
- adversarial critique
- long-horizon dependency simulation
- producer pass
- explicit confidence report

## Anti-patterns

The council fails when:

- every specialist produces a separate rewrite;
- the final answer feels averaged or committee-written;
- critique is generic rather than tied to the Intent Contract;
- specialists overrule canon or creator taste;
- more passes are added without measurable decision value;
- the orchestrator hides unresolved disagreements.

## Acceptance requirement

A deep execution is not approval-ready until the synthesis records:

- which passes ran;
- which findings changed the artifact;
- which findings were rejected and why;
- remaining uncertainties;
- confidence by dimension;
- downstream repository impact.
