# Genre Contract Standard

## Status

Canonical, repository-wide.

## Purpose

Define genre as an audience agreement rather than a rigid formula or prestige hierarchy.

## Governing principle

A genre contract specifies what kind of attention, expectation, pleasure, fear, laughter, release, recognition, or violation the project promises to manage.

Genre doctrine is descriptive and operational. It does not prohibit invention.

## Contract anatomy

Every active genre contract must define:

- audience promise;
- expected pleasures;
- required intelligibility;
- tolerated improbabilities;
- sincerity assumptions;
- pace expectations;
- escalation logic;
- character function expectations;
- payoff obligations;
- permitted violations;
- anti-goals;
- production implications.

## Contract states

Each convention must be marked as one of:

- **accepted** — delivered substantially as expected;
- **heightened** — delivered with increased scale, intensity, density, or stylization;
- **bent** — altered while preserving recognizability;
- **delayed** — withheld strategically and repaid later;
- **inverted** — meaningfully reversed;
- **rejected** — intentionally excluded;
- **unknown** — not yet resolved.

A convention may not be described as subverted unless the audience can first recognize the expectation being transformed.

## Contract hierarchy

For hybrid work, define:

1. primary genre;
2. secondary genre;
3. scene-level governing genre;
4. ending contract;
5. tie-breaking rule.

Example:

```yaml
genre_contract:
  primary: gross-out-comedy
  secondary:
    - body-horror
    - friendship-film
  ending_contract: friendship-film
  tie_breaker: protect the laugh unless doing so destroys character loyalty
```

## Comedy distinction

Comedy is not a single tone. Contracts may include:

- broad;
- deadpan;
- cringe;
- screwball;
- gross-out;
- slapstick;
- farce;
- black comedy;
- satire;
- parody;
- spoof;
- absurdist;
- romantic comedy;
- action comedy;
- stoner comedy;
- workplace comedy;
- mockumentary;
- children's comedy.

Each requires different timing, permission, targets, consequences, and realism tolerances.

## Audience permission

The project must teach the audience how to receive it through repeated evidence. Permission may be established through:

- opening image;
- performance register;
- consequence level;
- visual grammar;
- joke target;
- editing rhythm;
- music;
- reaction behaviour;
- early escalation;
- world response.

A project may shift its contract, but the shift must be legible and earned.

## Convention versus cliché

A convention is a recognizable functional element. A cliché is an element reproduced without sufficient specificity, transformation, timing, or consequence.

Do not remove a useful convention merely because it is familiar. Repair its execution or alter its relationship to the project.

## Diagnostic questions

1. What pleasure does the audience reasonably expect?
2. Which expectation is the project accepting, bending, or rejecting?
3. Has the audience been given permission for the project's register?
4. Is a convention functioning or merely present?
5. Does a violation create a stronger experience or only announce originality?
6. Which contract governs the ending?
7. Are tonal conflicts intentional, legible, and productive?

## Acceptance test

A genre contract passes when the audience promise is explicit enough to guide writing and evaluation while leaving room for project-specific invention.
