# Creative First Principles

## Status
Canonical, repository-wide.

## Purpose
Provide the smallest durable set of laws from which doctrine, diagnosis, workflows, and quality gates can derive. These laws are not genre preferences and do not require seriousness, realism, prestige, complexity, or restraint.

## Laws

### LAW-001 — Effects require causes
Material story events, decisions, reversals, jokes, scares, and payoffs must arise from identifiable causes, even when the surface is absurd.

### LAW-002 — Attention is selective
Characters and audiences cannot process everything. What is noticed, missed, emphasized, or withheld changes meaning.

### LAW-003 — Perception is interpreted
A stimulus does not create one automatic response. People appraise it through identity, memory, expectation, need, relationship, and context.

### LAW-004 — Action serves a function
Human behaviour attempts to obtain, protect, avoid, regulate, signal, conceal, test, or transform something.

### LAW-005 — Choice reveals priorities
Character is not a list of traits. It becomes legible through choices under pressure and the values sacrificed or protected.

### LAW-006 — Conflict requires incompatibility
Conflict emerges when goals, needs, interpretations, methods, identities, or available resources cannot coexist without cost.

### LAW-007 — Pressure narrows and distorts
Urgency, fear, desire, shame, status, fatigue, intoxication, confusion, and social exposure alter judgment and behaviour.

### LAW-008 — Consequence creates dramatic meaning
An event becomes dramatically meaningful through what it changes in knowledge, power, danger, relationship, identity, possibility, or audience expectation.

### LAW-009 — Audiences predict continuously
Viewers construct models of what is happening, what matters, what may happen next, and what kind of experience they are watching.

### LAW-010 — Surprise requires a prior model
A surprise works when it revises an audience prediction without making prior attention meaningless.

### LAW-011 — Repetition establishes pattern
Motifs, jokes, rituals, behaviours, objects, and structures gain meaning through recurrence, variation, escalation, or interruption.

### LAW-012 — Contrast produces perception
Differences in rhythm, scale, tone, silence, status, danger, sincerity, and visual density make qualities legible.

### LAW-013 — Compression increases force
Selection and concentration usually produce stronger dramatic effect than equal emphasis on everything.

### LAW-014 — Genre changes evaluation
A choice must be judged against creator intent, audience promise, and genre contract rather than a universal prestige standard.

### LAW-015 — Simplicity can be exact
A premise, character, joke, or plot may be intentionally simple, archetypal, broad, vulgar, cheap, or stupid while still being precisely executed.

### LAW-016 — Coherence is local before global
Even surreal, absurd, dreamlike, or deliberately incoherent work requires enough local pattern for the audience to understand how to receive it.

### LAW-017 — Production changes the work
Budget, cast, location, schedule, format, platform, performance, sound, edit, and physical execution are creative variables, not downstream paperwork.

### LAW-018 — Every recommendation has tradeoffs
An improvement in one dimension may weaken another. Agents must name the gained value, lost value, and governing priority.

### LAW-019 — Canon outranks convenience
Established project truth cannot be silently changed to solve a local writing problem.

### LAW-020 — Intent governs optimization
The system must improve the work the creator intends to make, not substitute a more respectable project.

## Derivation requirement
Every universal doctrine must identify the first principles it relies on. If a doctrine cannot trace its mechanism to one or more laws, it remains provisional.

## Conflict rule
First principles do not eliminate creative contradiction. When laws compete, use the project's declared intent, mode hierarchy, constraints, and success profile to determine priority.

## Acceptance test
A proposed law must be broad enough to apply across genres, precise enough to guide decisions, neutral toward prestige, and testable through observable creative consequences.
