# Cinematic Storytelling

## Screen stories are behaviour in time and space

Write what can be seen, heard, performed or inferred from concrete circumstances. Avoid prose that reports internal states unless translated into behaviour, image, rhythm, sound or consequence.

## Visual meaning

A visual detail earns emphasis when it:

- affects a choice;
- reveals attention or avoidance;
- changes status or geography;
- carries setup/payoff value;
- externalizes a relationship or thematic pressure;
- creates a production-relevant instruction.

Decoration without function slows the read and dilutes images that matter.

## Blocking and power

Physical position can dramatize:

- access and exclusion;
- intimacy and withdrawal;
- surveillance and exposure;
- dominance and dependency;
- belonging and alienation.

Track entrances, exits, thresholds, distance, eyelines and control of objects or pathways.

## Sound

Sound can lead image, bridge time, create false expectation, reveal off-screen space or make subjective experience physical. Use recurring sound motifs only when their meaning evolves.

Silence is not the absence of design. Specify it when the removal of expected sound changes attention or emotion.

## Production design

Environment should carry evidence of labour, history, class, belief, conflict and adaptation. Avoid generic "cinematic" spaces. Ask who made the space, who maintains it, who is unwelcome and what the occupants cannot afford to replace.

## Action writing

Action lines should be precise, playable and economical. Vary paragraph length according to attention and rhythm, not for artificial drama. Use fragments selectively. Do not direct every shot unless the shot itself is necessary to meaning.

## Subjectivity

A screenplay can express subjectivity through selective sensory access, distorted duration, sound, repeated imagery, spatial impossibility, omission and performance. Establish the rules of subjective sequences so ambiguity feels authored rather than accidental.

## Image systems

Build motifs through transformation:

1. introduce an image in a practical context;
2. repeat it with altered emotional meaning;
3. connect it to a choice or consequence;
4. pay it off in a form that reveals change.

Repeating symbols without changed meaning creates decoration, not resonance.

## Cinematic test

Remove the dialogue from a scene and summarize what remains understandable. Then remove action description and read only dialogue. The two channels should complement rather than duplicate each other.
