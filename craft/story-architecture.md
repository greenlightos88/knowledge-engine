# Story Architecture

## Story as pressure-driven change

A story is a chain of consequential choices made under increasing pressure. Plot is not a list of events. Plot is causality: because this happened, a character chooses that; because they choose that, the situation changes.

## Core architecture questions

A viable story can answer:

1. Who is forced to act?
2. What do they consciously want?
3. What deeper need or contradiction distorts how they pursue it?
4. What force actively resists them?
5. Why can they not walk away?
6. What becomes more costly after each major choice?
7. What truth, commitment or sacrifice becomes unavoidable?
8. Why can the ending happen only after this exact sequence?

## Causal spine

For each major beat, write:

> Because **[prior consequence]**, the protagonist chooses **[action]**, which causes **[new condition]**, forcing **[next harder choice]**.

If beats connect mainly with "and then," the structure is episodic. Repair the causal link or remove the beat.

## Progression of pressure

Escalation should evolve across dimensions:

- **difficulty:** the objective becomes harder;
- **cost:** success demands more sacrifice;
- **exposure:** hidden motives or vulnerabilities become visible;
- **compression:** time and alternatives diminish;
- **opposition intelligence:** resistance adapts;
- **moral consequence:** viable actions become ethically compromising;
- **identity:** the conflict threatens who the character believes they are.

Do not escalate every dimension at once. Sequence them to create rhythm.

## Turning points

A true turn changes the operating reality. It may alter:

- what the character knows;
- what they want;
- who has leverage;
- what action remains possible;
- how the audience interprets earlier events;
- what the character is willing to risk.

A reveal without changed behavior is information, not a turn.

## Setup and payoff

A setup earns attention by functioning in the present before paying off later. Strong setups are objects, habits, relationships, rules or choices that already matter. Payoffs should deliver recognition plus consequence, not merely remind the audience that the setup existed.

## Midpoint function

The midpoint is not automatically a twist or spectacle. Its job is to prevent the second half from repeating the first. It should meaningfully change strategy, understanding, alignment, scale or ownership of the conflict.

## Climax

The climax resolves the story's central dramatic argument through action. The protagonist must confront the deepest conflict using a choice their earlier self could not or would not make. External victory without internal cost or revelation usually feels mechanical.

## Ending test

The ending should answer:

- What became irreversible?
- What did the protagonist choose?
- What did that choice cost?
- What thematic question was complicated or resolved?
- What image, behavior or consequence lets the audience feel the answer without a speech?
