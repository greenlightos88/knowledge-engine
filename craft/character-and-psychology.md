# Character and Psychology

## Character is patterned choice

A character is not a biography, trait list or trauma inventory. Character becomes legible through repeated choices under changing pressure.

## Minimum viable character model

Define:

- **public objective:** what they are actively trying to achieve;
- **private need:** what would make them more whole, honest or capable;
- **misbelief:** the rule they use to survive that now causes damage;
- **fear:** what they believe will happen if they abandon that rule;
- **contradiction:** two authentic impulses that cannot be satisfied together;
- **strategy:** the habitual way they pursue control, safety, love or status;
- **threshold:** what they will not do at the beginning;
- **pressure response:** attack, flee, freeze, appease, perform, intellectualize, seduce, bargain, disappear or control;
- **cost signature:** who or what tends to pay for their coping strategy.

## Contradiction over quirk

A memorable character is often built from a coherent contradiction:

- deeply protective but emotionally withholding;
- socially fearless but privately dependent;
- morally rigorous except where family is concerned;
- highly competent in crisis but incapable of ordinary intimacy.

The contradiction must create decisions, not merely flavour.

## Desire hierarchy

Characters often operate with several desires at once:

1. immediate tactical objective;
2. scene-level interpersonal objective;
3. long-term external goal;
4. unconscious emotional need;
5. identity preservation.

Conflict sharpens when success at one level threatens another.

## Agency test

The protagonist should cause meaningful consequences through choices. Being attacked, selected, haunted, recruited or informed may begin a story, but sustained protagonism requires decisions that alter the path.

For each sequence ask:

- What choice did the character make?
- What alternative did they reject?
- What did that choice expose?
- What new problem did it create?

## Backstory discipline

Backstory matters only when it changes present behaviour, interpretation or consequence. Reveal it at the point of maximum dramatic leverage, not at the earliest available opportunity.

## Emotional credibility

Emotion should follow appraisal, not plot convenience. A character reacts according to what an event means to them, filtered through history, relationship, shame, hope and current capacity.

Avoid universal reactions. Two people can experience the same threat as humiliation, opportunity, abandonment, proof, liberation or inevitability.

## Arc design

An arc is a transformation in choice pattern, not simply a change in mood or knowledge.

Track:

- initial survival rule;
- early evidence that it works;
- accumulating collateral damage;
- moment the old rule becomes impossible to defend;
- costly experiment with a new behaviour;
- climax choice that proves or rejects transformation;
- ending consequence.

Flat arcs remain valid when a character's stable conviction transforms the world or reveals the cost of refusing transformation.

## Relationship design

Every important relationship should contain:

- a shared history or immediate dependency;
- asymmetrical knowledge or power;
- a thing each wants from the other;
- a thing each refuses to give;
- a pattern of misrecognition;
- a pressure point capable of changing the relationship.

Relationships become dramatic when objectives cross, not merely when personalities differ.
