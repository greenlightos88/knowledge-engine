# Pacing, Tension and Revelation

## Pacing is rate of meaningful change

Fast cutting is not automatically fast pacing. Slow scenes can feel urgent when choices narrow, danger approaches or interpretation changes. Pacing depends on how frequently the audience receives consequential shifts and how long anticipation is sustained before release.

## Rhythm

Build rhythm through contrast:

- compression and expansion;
- action and aftermath;
- certainty and doubt;
- public pressure and private consequence;
- noise and silence;
- pursuit and temporary refuge.

Uniform intensity numbs the audience. Recovery beats should deepen attachment, consequence or dread rather than pause the story.

## Tension equation

Tension grows from a valued outcome, credible threat, incomplete control, narrowing time or options, and uncertainty about the result. The audience must understand enough to anticipate consequences.

## Suspense versus surprise

- **Suspense:** viewers anticipate a possible event and endure the approach.
- **Surprise:** viewers encounter an event without anticipation.
- **Dread:** viewers believe an event may be inevitable but do not know its form or timing.
- **Curiosity:** viewers recognize a meaningful gap and want interpretation.

Choose deliberately. Do not call confusion mystery.

## Information control

Map who knows what and when:

- audience ahead of character creates suspense;
- character ahead of audience creates curiosity or mistrust;
- equal discovery creates identification and shock;
- conflicting interpretations create dramatic irony and debate.

A reveal should change meaning, strategy, allegiance or consequence. A fact that changes nothing is trivia.

## Escalation ladder

For a sequence, escalate by changing at least one of:

1. objective;
2. obstacle intelligence;
3. cost of failure;
4. exposure;
5. time pressure;
6. moral compromise;
7. physical geography;
8. relationship leverage.

Avoid repeating the same beat with a louder performance.

## Horror pressure

Fear becomes durable when the threat attacks a character-specific vulnerability and obeys intelligible dramatic rules. Withhold appearance more readily than consequence. Let the audience study patterns, form hypotheses and fear being correct.

## Action pressure

Action is a chain of tactical decisions. Geography, objectives, capability limits and consequences must remain legible. Spectacle should reveal character, alter strategy or create irreversible cost.

## Pacing audit

For every sequence mark:

- new question raised;
- prior question answered or transformed;
- choice made;
- consequence delivered;
- intensity change;
- relationship or power shift;
- setup planted or paid off.

Long stretches without one of these signals usually indicate drift.
