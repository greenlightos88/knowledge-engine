# Scene Mechanics

## Scene definition

A scene is a unit of active pressure in which at least one participant pursues an objective and the conditions change. Atmosphere, exposition or spectacle may be present, but none is a substitute for dramatic movement.

## Scene card

Before drafting, define:

- **entry condition:** what is already in motion;
- **point-of-view pressure:** whose experience governs the scene;
- **objective:** what they want before the scene ends;
- **opposition:** who or what actively complicates that objective;
- **tactic:** how they initially try to win;
- **hidden information:** what is concealed, misunderstood or unequally known;
- **turn:** what changes the operating reality;
- **choice:** what someone does because of the turn;
- **exit condition:** what is now more dangerous, intimate, constrained or irreversible;
- **audience effect:** what viewers should feel or anticipate next.

## Enter late, leave changed

Enter after routine has begun and before the decisive pressure becomes obvious. Leave once the consequence is clear enough to propel the next beat. Do not overstay to summarize what the audience just witnessed.

## Conflict is not argument

Conflict exists when objectives cannot all be satisfied. It may appear as politeness, seduction, silence, procedure, avoidance, hospitality, humour, labour or violence.

## Beat progression

A useful scene generally moves through tactical adjustments:

1. approach;
2. resistance;
3. adaptation;
4. exposure or reversal;
5. consequential choice.

Do not force this into a rigid formula. The essential requirement is adaptation: pressure changes behaviour.

## Value change

Track a meaningful value from opening to closing, such as:

- safe → exposed;
- allied → suspicious;
- ignorant → implicated;
- powerful → dependent;
- controlled → volatile;
- hopeful → committed;
- trapped → morally compromised.

A scene can change more than one value, but at least one should move clearly.

## Exposition placement

Deliver information when it becomes ammunition, obstacle, temptation, consequence or re-interpretation. Information that changes nothing belongs elsewhere or should be removed.

## Visual substitution test

Before writing dialogue, ask what can be conveyed through:

- choice of task;
- spatial distance;
- interruption;
- possession or handling of an object;
- refusal to look;
- who serves whom;
- who controls the doorway, vehicle, weapon, money or information;
- a repeated behaviour that changes meaning.

## Scene failure patterns

- no one wants anything specific;
- all participants agree too quickly;
- the scene repeats information or emotion from the previous scene;
- conflict remains at one intensity and one tactic;
- the turn is externally imposed without a character response;
- dialogue explains subtext;
- the scene could be removed without changing later choices.

## Rewrite order

Repair scenes in this order:

1. function;
2. objective and opposition;
3. turn and choice;
4. information control;
5. physical behaviour;
6. dialogue;
7. sentence polish.
