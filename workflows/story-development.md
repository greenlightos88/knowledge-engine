# Story Development Workflow

Use this workflow for a new concept, major re-architecture or project restart.

## Phase 1 — Mandate

Produce a one-page development brief containing:

- format and intended audience;
- premise in causal terms;
- protagonist and central pressure;
- genre promise and tonal boundaries;
- production reality;
- target emotional experience;
- non-negotiables;
- unresolved creative decisions.

**Gate:** the concept must contain a person forced into consequential action, not only a world, mood or lore system.

## Phase 2 — Dramatic engine

Define:

- protagonist objective, need, misbelief and threshold;
- opposition objective and adaptive strategy;
- central relationship pressure;
- stakes and reason walking away is impossible;
- source of repeatable conflict;
- thematic question expressed as competing values.

**Output:** dramatic engine memo.

## Phase 3 — Ending first

Design the climax choice, cost and final consequence before expanding the middle. Identify what the protagonist must become willing to do and what setup makes that choice inevitable.

**Output:** ending contract, subject to revision but never ignored.

## Phase 4 — Causal architecture

Build the major sequence chain using because/therefore logic. For each sequence define objective, obstacle, turn, choice, consequence, audience question and setup/payoff activity.

**Gate:** remove sequences connected mainly by chronology.

## Phase 5 — Character system

Create character files only for roles that affect choices, pressure or production understanding. Map relationships as exchanges of leverage and unmet need.

**Gate:** no character is approved from traits and biography alone.

## Phase 6 — Information architecture

Map:

- hidden truth;
- who knows each part;
- audience knowledge by sequence;
- intended hypotheses;
- reveal consequences;
- mysteries that remain open by design.

**Gate:** confusion cannot be used as suspense.

## Phase 7 — Scene design

Create scene cards. Verify entry condition, objective, opposition, tactic, turn, choice and exit condition before prose drafting.

## Phase 8 — Draft

Draft in sequence-sized batches. Preserve momentum and voice, but do not postpone structural defects until a polish pass.

## Phase 9 — Multi-pass diagnosis

Run separate passes in this order:

1. causality and agency;
2. character and relationships;
3. scene necessity and turns;
4. pacing and information;
5. cinematic execution;
6. dialogue and voice;
7. continuity and production fitness;
8. anti-pattern scan.

Do not combine all notes into vague "make it stronger" feedback.

## Phase 10 — Controlled revision

Rank defects by dependency. Repair architecture before dialogue, and dialogue before line polish. Track each approved change and rescan affected downstream material.

## Phase 11 — Lock and package

Update canon, continuity, decision log and artifact status. Produce the version appropriate for the next reader: creator draft, actor draft, producer draft, pitch material or production package.
