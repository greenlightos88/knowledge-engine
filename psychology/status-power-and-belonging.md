# Status, Power, and Belonging

## Purpose

Design social behaviour around rank, access, legitimacy, dependence, and fear of exclusion.

## Principle

Status is not merely wealth or authority. It is the character's perceived position within a specific group, interaction, or hierarchy.

Power is the capacity to shape options, consequences, information, resources, attention, or belonging.

## Mechanism

```text
social cue
→ rank and belonging appraised
→ gain or threat detected
→ display, challenge, submission, coalition, concealment, or exit
→ others respond
→ hierarchy updates
```

## Status dimensions

A character may hold different status across:

- formal authority;
- expertise;
- physical threat;
- wealth or access;
- moral legitimacy;
- attractiveness or desirability;
- cultural fluency;
- family position;
- group history;
- control of information;
- emotional independence;
- ability to grant or deny belonging.

Do not collapse these into one ladder. Status can be mixed, unstable, and contested.

## Behavioural expression

Status appears through:

- who interrupts and who yields;
- who chooses location, timing, topic, and terms;
- who explains and who expects understanding;
- who can joke at whose expense;
- who asks permission;
- who controls silence;
- who handles objects, space, money, or evidence;
- who is watched before others decide;
- who can violate norms without punishment;
- who performs belonging and who assumes it.

## Failure modes and repair

### Power as title only

A leader has authority in exposition but no effect on others' options.

**Repair:** show access, consequence, attention, information, or resource control.

### Static hierarchy

Status remains unchanged despite failure, revelation, courage, betrayal, or dependence.

**Repair:** let consequential behaviour update rank and legitimacy.

### Dominance clichés

Power is shown only through shouting, threats, or physical intimidation.

**Repair:** use calm expectation, selective attention, procedural control, social permission, or the ability to make others wait.

### Belonging without cost

Inclusion or exclusion carries no behavioural consequence.

**Repair:** define what the group grants: safety, identity, livelihood, history, legitimacy, access, or love.

## Diagnostic questions

- Who can impose a cost without asking?
- Who needs whose recognition?
- What happens if the character loses standing?
- Which form of status is visible and which is hidden?
- Who has procedural power despite low social rank?
- How does the hierarchy change by the scene's end?

## Acceptance test

The mechanism passes when power shapes available choices, status is relationship-specific, belonging carries meaningful value, and interaction updates hierarchy or legitimacy.
