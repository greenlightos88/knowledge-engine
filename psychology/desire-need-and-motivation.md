# Desire, Need, and Motivation

## Principle

A character becomes dramatically legible when immediate wants, deeper needs, feared costs, and self-protective strategies collide.

Desire is not a biography statement. It is pressure expressed as pursuit.

## Four-layer model

### Immediate objective

What the character is trying to obtain, prevent, hide, prove, or escape in the current scene.

### Sustained desire

What they pursue across a sequence or story: belonging, control, recognition, safety, revenge, truth, freedom, restoration, transcendence.

### Unacknowledged need

What must change in their relationship to self, others, or reality for the central pattern to stop repeating.

### Feared cost

What they believe honesty, change, intimacy, failure, or surrender would cost them.

The need must not simply be the morally correct opposite of the want. It should emerge from the character's causal pattern.

## Motivation construction

A strong motivation contains:

- a valued outcome;
- a reason it matters now;
- a barrier;
- a cost of failure;
- a cost of success;
- a preferred strategy;
- a line the character believes they will not cross.

Pressure becomes dramatic when the preferred strategy stops working.

## Common failures

### Abstract wanting

The character wants happiness, justice, love, or success without a concrete behaviour.

**Repair:** Convert the abstraction into an immediate, observable pursuit.

### Plot obedience

The character acts because the story requires movement.

**Repair:** Trace the choice to appraisal, stakes, available options, and prior strategy.

### Single-motive purity

Every action serves one clean motive.

**Repair:** Add competing incentives. A character can rescue someone while seeking forgiveness, status, control, or proof of worth.

### Declared need

The character accurately explains their own deepest problem early.

**Repair:** Let the need appear through repeated cost, failed coping, and relationship damage before it becomes articulate.

## Scene application

Before drafting, state:

```text
OBJECTIVE:
WHY NOW:
VISIBLE TACTIC:
HIDDEN MOTIVE:
FEARED EXPOSURE:
COST OF FAILURE:
COST OF SUCCESS:
POINT OF ADAPTATION:
```

## Acceptance test

Motivation passes when the character's behaviour remains understandable even when it is morally wrong, strategically flawed, or emotionally contradictory.
