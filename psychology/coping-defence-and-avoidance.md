# Coping, Defence, and Avoidance

## Purpose

Translate psychological protection into recurring behaviour that creates both short-term function and long-term dramatic cost.

## Principle

People protect themselves before they transform. A coping strategy survives because it once worked, still works in limited contexts, or protects a value the character cannot yet risk losing.

## Mechanism

```text
pain, threat, or conflict
→ intolerable meaning
→ protective strategy
→ immediate relief or control
→ hidden cost
→ repetition
→ increasing consequence
→ adaptation or collapse
```

## Core distinction

- **Coping:** manages distress or demand.
- **Defence:** protects identity from painful recognition.
- **Avoidance:** prevents contact with feared feeling, fact, choice, or consequence.

These may overlap, but the dramatic function should be clear.

## Common strategies

- intellectualization;
- humour;
- control and planning;
- denial or minimization;
- blame or projection;
- emotional withdrawal;
- caretaking;
- aggression;
- perfectionism;
- appeasement;
- compulsive work;
- ritual;
- substance use;
- sensation seeking;
- secrecy;
- moral certainty;
- detachment disguised as independence.

Do not reduce a character to one defence. Define a preferred strategy, secondary strategy, and breakdown strategy.

## Construction model

For each major strategy, specify:

- what it protects;
- what triggers it;
- what relief it provides;
- what relationship it damages;
- what information it prevents the character from receiving;
- what behaviour appears when the strategy fails;
- what healthier adaptation would require them to risk.

## Failure modes and repair

### Coping as decoration

A character drinks, jokes, works, or lashes out without a clear protective function.

**Repair:** identify the intolerable meaning being managed and the relief created.

### Trauma as personality

All character behaviour is explained by a wound.

**Repair:** restore appetite, humour, competence, pleasure, contradiction, values, social roles, and choices unrelated to the wound.

### Instant therapeutic insight

The character correctly names the defence and abandons it after one conversation.

**Repair:** require behavioural evidence, cost, failed repetition, and a choice that risks the protected value.

### Strategy without payoff

The defence creates no short-term advantage, making continued use implausible.

**Repair:** show why it works before showing why it fails.

### Strategy without cost

The coping pattern remains harmless.

**Repair:** connect it to missed intimacy, distorted judgment, moral compromise, delayed action, or preventable consequence.

## Diagnostic questions

- What pain is the character avoiding right now?
- Why is this strategy preferable to honesty?
- What does the strategy allow them to accomplish?
- What does it make impossible?
- Who recognizes the pattern before the character does?
- What pressure would force escalation or adaptation?

## Acceptance test

The pattern passes when it is understandable, useful, costly, relationship-specific, and capable of generating both scene tactics and story consequence.
