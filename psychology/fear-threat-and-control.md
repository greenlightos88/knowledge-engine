# Fear, Threat, and Control

## Purpose

Design fear as a specific threat model that changes attention, behaviour, choice, morality, and pacing.

## Principle

Fear does not arise from danger alone. It arises from what the character believes may be harmed, how likely the harm feels, how severe it seems, and whether control appears possible.

## Mechanism

```text
perceived threat
→ valued target identified
→ probability and severity estimated
→ control assessed
→ attention narrows
→ protective impulse forms
→ behaviour selected
→ consequence updates threat model
```

## Required inputs

- **Threat object:** what appears dangerous.
- **Valued target:** body, attachment, status, identity, control, certainty, belonging, morality, or future.
- **Probability estimate:** how likely harm feels.
- **Severity estimate:** how catastrophic it feels.
- **Control estimate:** whether action seems useful.
- **Time horizon:** immediate, approaching, chronic, or imagined.
- **Visibility:** whether others can see the fear.
- **Forbidden response:** what reaction the character refuses to permit.

Fear intensifies when uncertainty is high, control is low, and the threatened value is central to identity.

## Behavioural outputs

Fear may produce:

- scanning, fixation, or missed information;
- freezing, delay, or dissociation;
- escape planning;
- aggression used to restore control;
- humour used to reduce exposure;
- ritual, checking, rehearsal, or over-preparation;
- denial or minimization;
- appeasement;
- information hoarding;
- reckless action to end uncertainty;
- moral narrowing under survival pressure.

Response is context-dependent. Do not assign one permanent fight, flight, freeze, or fawn type to a character.

## Dramatic forms

### Dread

Expected harm remains unconfirmed. Partial evidence and shrinking options sustain it.

### Panic

Threat overwhelms planning. Attention narrows and action becomes immediate.

### Terror

Severe harm feels imminent while meaningful control collapses.

### Chronic fear

Threat becomes normalized. The character may appear calm because vigilance has become routine.

### Moral fear

The threatened loss is identity: becoming someone despised, betraying a value, or discovering one's capacity for harm.

## Failure modes and repair

### Generic fear performance

Gasps, trembling, backing away, and explicit declarations substitute for character-specific response.

**Repair:** derive behaviour from the valued target, control estimate, social audience, and learned strategy.

### Threat without cost

Danger exists, but nothing important to the character is meaningfully at risk.

**Repair:** connect the threat to a specific attachment, identity, future, duty, secret, or line the character cannot bear to cross.

### Constant maximum intensity

The character remains visibly terrified without adaptation.

**Repair:** vary threat certainty, available control, social concealment, fatigue, and habituation. Let fear become strategy before it becomes collapse.

### Arbitrary irrationality

The character makes a foolish choice solely to enable plot.

**Repair:** establish the appraisal that makes the choice locally rational: incomplete evidence, time pressure, shame, attachment, prior success, or a worse feared alternative.

### Fear explained instead of dramatized

Dialogue names the threat, history, and emotion while behaviour remains unchanged.

**Repair:** alter attention, tactics, speech, timing, distance, and willingness to accept cost.

## Scene application

Define:

```text
THREAT AS PERCEIVED:
VALUED TARGET:
WHAT THE CHARACTER KNOWS:
WHAT THEY MISREAD:
CONTROL AVAILABLE:
CONTROL THEY IMAGINE:
PROTECTIVE STRATEGY:
FORBIDDEN RESPONSE:
COST CREATED BY THE STRATEGY:
HOW THE THREAT MODEL CHANGES:
```

## Diagnostic questions

- What exactly is this character afraid will happen?
- What would that event mean about them or their world?
- What detail becomes disproportionately important under fear?
- Which option feels unavailable, and why?
- How does fear change the character's moral range?
- Does the response differ depending on who is watching?
- What consequence confirms, disproves, or mutates the threat model?

## Acceptance test

The fear mechanism passes when behaviour is specific to the character's appraisal, the threatened value is clear, the coping strategy creates consequence, and escalation changes either certainty, control, severity, or sacrifice rather than merely increasing noise.
