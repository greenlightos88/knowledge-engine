# Appraisal and Meaning

## Principle

Emotion follows the character's interpretation of an event, not the event alone.

The same silence may mean rejection, safety, punishment, respect, concealment, or nothing. The character's history, goal, identity, relationship, and current state determine the appraisal.

## Dramatic model

For any emotionally meaningful beat, identify:

1. **What happened?** Observable stimulus.
2. **What did the character notice?** Attention is selective.
3. **What do they think it means?** Interpretation.
4. **What value is threatened or offered?** Safety, attachment, status, identity, control, justice, belonging, desire.
5. **How certain are they?** Certainty changes behaviour.
6. **What response feels available?** Perceived options matter more than objective options.

## On-page expression

Do not automatically state the appraisal. Express it through:

- what detail captures attention;
- what question is asked or avoided;
- a wrong inference;
- changed distance or posture;
- an attempt to verify, control, joke, attack, leave, conceal, or repair;
- a choice that would make little sense under a different interpretation.

## Common failures

### Event-equals-emotion

Everyone reacts to death with visible grief, danger with panic, praise with pleasure, or betrayal with anger.

**Repair:** Specify the personal meaning. A death may create relief, administrative focus, guilt, disbelief, freedom, or fear of inheritance.

### Authorial omniscience

The character reacts to information the audience knows but they do not.

**Repair:** Restrict behaviour to available evidence and the character's current model.

### Convenient misunderstanding

A false interpretation exists only to prolong plot and would disappear after one ordinary question.

**Repair:** Give the character a reason not to ask: shame, status, danger, pride, loyalty, prior evidence, or a belief that asking would expose them.

### Emotion labels without mechanism

The page says someone is devastated, furious, or anxious without showing what changed in their perception or choices.

**Repair:** Replace the label with altered attention, behaviour, strategy, or error.

## Diagnostic questions

- Why does this event mean this particular thing to this person?
- What would another character infer differently?
- Which prior experience is shaping the interpretation?
- What evidence is the character ignoring?
- What does the appraisal make them do now?
- How will consequence update or harden the appraisal?

## Acceptance test

The beat passes when a reader can infer a specific internal meaning from behaviour without requiring explanatory dialogue, and when that meaning causes a consequential choice.
