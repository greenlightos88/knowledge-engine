# Psychology Gate

## Purpose

Reject character behaviour that is fluent, dramatic, or convenient but psychologically uncaused.

Apply this gate to principal characters, major reversals, emotionally decisive scenes, relationship turns, confessions, betrayals, sacrifices, and breakdowns.

## Rating

Score each category:

- **2 — PASS:** specific, causally legible, and dramatically productive.
- **1 — REVISE:** present but generic, incomplete, or weakly integrated.
- **0 — FAIL:** absent, contradictory, or dependent on plot convenience.

A psychologically important artifact requires:

- no automatic-fail condition;
- no zero in appraisal, motivation, or decision causality;
- at least **18/24** overall.

## 1. Appraisal

- Does behaviour arise from what the character believes the event means?
- Is that interpretation specific to history, identity, values, and available evidence?
- Could another character plausibly interpret the same event differently?

## 2. Motivation

- Is the immediate objective observable?
- Is there a credible reason it matters now?
- Are hidden motive, feared cost, and competing incentive present where relevant?

## 3. Emotional mechanism

- Is emotion connected to a threatened or offered value?
- Does emotion alter attention, tactics, speech, timing, or moral range?
- Is the emotion dramatized rather than merely named?

## 4. Coping and defence

- Does the character use a protective strategy with short-term benefit?
- Does that strategy create cost, distortion, or relationship damage?
- Is the pattern specific rather than a trauma cliché?

## 5. Identity, shame, and guilt

- Is the threatened identity or violated value clear?
- Does social audience affect behaviour?
- Are concealment, confession, repair, punishment, and forgiveness kept distinct?

## 6. Attachment and relationship regulation

- Does behaviour change depending on who is present?
- Do closeness, dependence, autonomy, trust, or abandonment carry specific risk?
- Does the interaction update the relationship rather than merely display it?

## 7. Status, power, and belonging

- Do power and rank shape available choices?
- Is belonging materially or emotionally valuable?
- Does interaction alter hierarchy, legitimacy, access, or dependence?

## 8. Decision causality

- Is the choice locally rational from the character's perspective?
- Are perceived options constrained by information, time, identity, fear, values, and relationships?
- Does the decision create irreversible or strategically meaningful consequence?

## 9. Contradiction

- Do competing needs or values produce genuine tension?
- Is inconsistency functional rather than random?
- Does pressure reveal which value dominates and at what cost?

## 10. Behavioural specificity

- Are psychology and emotion visible through action, omission, posture, attention, ritual, error, or sacrifice?
- Could the behaviour belong to this character and relationship rather than any generic protagonist?

## 11. Change model

- What evidence could update the character's belief or strategy?
- Has change been earned through consequence rather than explanation?
- If the character does not change, is the hardening of the pattern equally causal?

## 12. Dramatic productivity

- Does psychology generate conflict, adaptation, leverage, sacrifice, or consequence?
- Would removing the psychological mechanism materially weaken the scene or arc?

## Automatic fail conditions

The artifact fails automatically when:

- a major decision exists only because the plot requires it;
- a character reacts to information they do not possess;
- an obvious alternative is ignored without psychological, temporal, social, or material constraint;
- trauma substitutes for a complete personality;
- one conversation produces unearned permanent healing;
- every character displays the same emotional fluency;
- emotion is named repeatedly but does not alter behaviour;
- relationship history has no effect on present conduct;
- status and power are asserted but never shape options;
- a reversal lacks new evidence, cost, threshold, or value conflict.

## Ordered repair sequence

1. Reconstruct the stimulus and available evidence.
2. Define the character's appraisal and threatened value.
3. Clarify immediate objective and competing motives.
4. Identify the protective strategy and why it persists.
5. Define relationship, status, identity, and audience effects.
6. Restrict perceived options under current pressure.
7. Rebuild the choice and consequence.
8. Translate the mechanism into observable behaviour.
9. Remove explanatory dialogue that duplicates the behaviour.
10. Re-run this gate and all dependent scene, dialogue, human-writing, and continuity gates.
