# Attachment and Relational Regulation

## Purpose

Design how characters seek safety, closeness, autonomy, reassurance, and control through relationships.

## Principle

People do not behave the same way with everyone. Relationships regulate emotion, identity, risk tolerance, attention, and choice.

## Mechanism

```text
need for closeness or autonomy
→ cue interpreted through relationship history
→ security or threat assessed
→ proximity strategy selected
→ partner responds
→ pattern stabilizes, escalates, or changes
```

## Relational variables

Define:

- desired closeness;
- feared dependence;
- feared abandonment;
- trust level;
- power imbalance;
- history of repair or betrayal;
- reassurance strategy;
- protest behaviour;
- withdrawal behaviour;
- boundary style;
- what each person regulates for the other.

Avoid treating attachment labels as fixed diagnoses. Use observable patterns under specific pressure.

## Common patterns

- pursuing reassurance;
- testing loyalty;
- withdrawing before rejection;
- controlling logistics to reduce uncertainty;
- offering care instead of asking for care;
- using competence to earn belonging;
- creating conflict to force proof of commitment;
- minimizing need to preserve autonomy;
- confusing intensity with safety;
- accepting mistreatment to prevent loss;
- refusing repair because repair would admit dependence.

## Failure modes and repair

### Relationship as biography

The connection is described but does not regulate behaviour.

**Repair:** show how the presence, absence, approval, silence, or threat of the other person changes strategy.

### Identical behaviour across relationships

The character speaks and acts the same with parent, lover, rival, subordinate, and stranger.

**Repair:** define audience-specific risk, power, dependence, and history.

### Conflict without attachment value

Characters argue, but losing the relationship would not matter.

**Repair:** identify what emotional, practical, social, or identity function the relationship provides.

### Instant trust repair

One apology restores security.

**Repair:** separate apology, accountability, changed behaviour, verification, and renewed vulnerability.

## Scene application

```text
RELATIONSHIP VALUE:
CURRENT SECURITY LEVEL:
CUE INTERPRETED:
FEARED LOSS:
PROXIMITY OR DISTANCE STRATEGY:
PROTEST BEHAVIOUR:
WHAT CANNOT BE ASKED DIRECTLY:
PARTNER RESPONSE:
PATTERN REINFORCED OR DISRUPTED:
NEW RELATIONAL CONSEQUENCE:
```

## Diagnostic questions

- What does each person need from the other beyond plot function?
- What behaviour seeks closeness while pretending not to?
- What behaviour seeks distance while inviting pursuit?
- Who can dysregulate or calm whom, and how?
- What does vulnerability cost in this relationship?
- What evidence would make trust rationally increase or decrease?

## Acceptance test

The relationship passes when each person's behaviour changes in the other's presence, closeness and autonomy carry specific risks, and interaction updates trust, dependence, power, or willingness to be known.
