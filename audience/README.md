# Audience Cognition Engine

The Audience Cognition Engine models what viewers attend to, predict, misunderstand, feel, remember and revise while experiencing a story. It prevents the system from treating audience response as an accidental by-product of technically correct writing.

## Core state model

At any meaningful beat, track:

- **Attention:** what currently dominates perception.
- **Orientation:** what the audience believes is happening.
- **Question:** what outcome or meaning remains unresolved.
- **Prediction:** what the audience expects next.
- **Confidence:** how certain that prediction feels.
- **Alignment:** whose experience or objective organizes the moment.
- **Emotion:** what action tendency is being activated.
- **Information gap:** what characters and audience know differently.
- **Revision:** what new evidence changes interpretation.
- **Memory trace:** what image, action, line or sound should remain available for later payoff.

## Primary systems

- `attention/` — salience, pacing, overload, habituation and reorientation.
- `prediction/` — expectation, pattern, uncertainty, setup and violation.
- `comprehension/` — causal clarity, ambiguity, confusion and delayed understanding.
- `alignment/` — empathy, identification, access, distance and moral tension.
- `emotion/` — dread, grief, relief, desire, disgust, wonder, humour and awe.
- `memory/` — motif recognition, callbacks, reveal readiness and payoff retention.
- `meaning/` — thematic inference, symbolism, contradiction and ending interpretation.

## Design rules

- The audience must have enough information to form a prediction before surprise can function.
- Confusion is useful only when the intended question remains legible.
- Suspense requires anticipated consequence; mystery requires an interpretable information gap.
- Empathy does not require approval. It requires access to pressure, value or vulnerability.
- Repetition should alter meaning, intensity or context each time.
- Payoff strength depends on setup visibility, memory retention and transformed significance.
- Emotional intensity requires modulation. Constant maximum pressure produces habituation.
- A reveal must change interpretation or future action, not merely add information.

## Beat-level diagnostic

For each major beat, ask:

1. What is the audience looking at or listening for?
2. What do they think the moment means?
3. What do they expect?
4. What uncertainty keeps them engaged?
5. What new evidence arrives?
6. How does it revise prediction, feeling or moral judgment?
7. What should remain in memory?
8. What does the next beat exploit?

## Failure conditions

Reject sequences that:

- conceal the basic dramatic question;
- substitute withholding for mystery;
- reveal information before the audience can value it;
- rely on characters explaining stakes already demonstrated;
- create surprise by violating established causality;
- overload names, lore or objectives beyond working memory;
- manipulate emotion without credible character or consequence;
- repeat the same emotional state without transformation;
- end without changing prediction, interpretation or commitment.

## Integration

Audience cognition must be checked during outlining, scene design, revision, trailer extraction and final packaging. It does not override canon or character truth; it verifies whether those truths are being delivered as an experience.
