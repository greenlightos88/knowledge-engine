# Human Mechanics Engine

The Human Mechanics Engine explains how internal processes produce observable behaviour under specific conditions. It exists to prevent characters from behaving according to plot convenience, genre shorthand or author explanation.

## Required causal model

```text
Situation
→ selective attention
→ appraisal
→ prediction
→ bodily and emotional response
→ competing motives
→ available options
→ decision threshold
→ behaviour
→ social interpretation
→ consequence
→ learning or reinforcement
```

Not every beat must be shown, but major behaviour must remain compatible with this chain.

## Domains

- `perception/` — sensory selection, viewpoint, salience and misperception.
- `attention/` — focus, distraction, vigilance, habituation and overload.
- `memory/` — encoding, recall, association, distortion and identity memory.
- `prediction/` — expectation, uncertainty, control and error correction.
- `appraisal/` — personal meaning assigned to events.
- `emotion/` — action tendencies, regulation and mixed states.
- `motivation/` — desire, need, conflict, inhibition and persistence.
- `decision/` — tradeoffs, thresholds, bias, time pressure and irreversible choice.
- `coping/` — avoidance, defence, adaptation, ritual and self-protection.
- `identity/` — self-concept, role, shame, dignity and dissonance.
- `attachment/` — proximity, trust, abandonment, care and relational regulation.
- `status/` — rank, belonging, dominance, submission and social threat.
- `language/` — concealment, implication, persuasion, repair and miscommunication.
- `groups/` — conformity, coalition, diffusion, loyalty and collective behaviour.
- `violence/` — threat assessment, escalation, inhibition, survival and aftermath.
- `meaning/` — purpose, morality, ritual, grief, hope and transformation.

## Application contract

Before writing decisive character behaviour, resolve:

1. What does the character notice that another person may not?
2. What meaning do they assign to it?
3. What outcome do they predict?
4. What do they want immediately?
5. What deeper need or identity is implicated?
6. What option feels unavailable, intolerable or invisible?
7. What cost are they willing to accept?
8. What behaviour protects them in the short term?
9. What consequence does that behaviour create?
10. What will the character learn, deny or reinforce afterward?

## Failure conditions

Reject behaviour when it:

- exists only to move the plot;
- contradicts established perception, motive or capacity without pressure sufficient to explain the change;
- reduces emotion to a label;
- treats trauma as a complete personality;
- gives every character equal self-awareness;
- assumes people say what they mean;
- confuses intensity with psychological credibility;
- removes social, cultural or material context;
- has no consequence for future prediction or behaviour.

## Integration

Human-mechanics doctrine feeds character design, dialogue, scene architecture, performance notes, continuity, audience cognition and the Psychology Gate. Changes to a major character mechanism require a dependency scan across all of those systems.
