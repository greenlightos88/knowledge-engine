# Attention, Salience and Selection

## Purpose

Determine what the audience notices, what they ignore and whether narrative emphasis matches narrative importance.

## Mechanism

Attention is selective. Audiences do not process every available detail equally. Selection is influenced by contrast, motion, novelty, threat, faces, sound change, goal relevance, emotional charge and prior expectation.

## Core rules

1. The most salient element becomes the working subject of the moment.
2. Salience can be visual, verbal, sonic, emotional, spatial or causal.
3. Relevance sustains attention longer than novelty alone.
4. Competing high-salience elements divide comprehension.
5. Background information becomes memorable when it later proves causally useful.
6. Deliberate misdirection must preserve a fair path to the hidden truth.

## Diagnostic signs

- viewers miss a setup;
- readers focus on an irrelevant prop;
- emotional beats are buried under plot information;
- action geography becomes unclear;
- dialogue competes with spectacle;
- a reveal depends on a detail that received no usable emphasis.

## Diagnostic procedure

For each beat, record:

- intended focus;
- strongest actual stimulus;
- audience question;
- competing stimuli;
- required retained information.

If intended focus and strongest actual stimulus differ, classify the mismatch as deliberate, harmless or destructive.

## Repair strategies

- remove competing information;
- delay secondary exposition;
- increase contrast around the intended element;
- connect the information to a character objective;
- repeat through changed context rather than duplication;
- use reaction, consequence or spatial isolation to mark importance;
- move the setup closer to its use when long-term memory is unnecessary.

## Tradeoffs

- Increased emphasis can make a reveal predictable.
- Reduced detail can flatten atmosphere or realism.
- Repetition improves retention but may feel mechanical.
- Misdirection improves surprise but can damage trust when unfair.

## Dependencies

Influences:

- comprehension;
- memory encoding;
- suspense;
- comedy timing;
- action readability;
- reveal fairness.
