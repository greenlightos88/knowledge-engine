# Prediction, Expectation and Surprise

## Purpose

Design what the audience expects, how strongly they expect it and what happens when the story confirms, delays, complicates or violates that prediction.

## Mechanism

Audiences continuously form models from genre, pattern, character behaviour, visual emphasis and causal setup. Surprise occurs when new information forces model revision. Satisfaction depends on both novelty and recoverability.

## Core rules

1. Prediction requires a legible pattern or causal path.
2. Suspense often strengthens when the audience can predict danger but not timing, method or outcome.
3. Surprise is strongest when the prior model was reasonable and the revised model explains more evidence.
4. A twist must alter meaning, consequence or future action—not merely disclose trivia.
5. Genre expectations may be fulfilled, delayed, inverted or broken, but the chosen move must serve the declared experience.
6. Randomness produces shock; structure produces revelation.

## Prediction states

- no model: the audience lacks enough information;
- weak hypothesis: several outcomes appear plausible;
- dominant prediction: one outcome appears most likely;
- dramatic irony: the audience predicts from information a character lacks;
- false confidence: the story has intentionally over-supported a wrong model;
- revised model: new evidence reorganizes earlier information.

## Diagnostic signs

- reveal feels obvious long before intended;
- twist feels arbitrary;
- mystery has no usable hypotheses;
- suspense is absent because danger is undefined;
- genre payoff feels withheld rather than transformed;
- audience cannot explain why the reveal makes sense afterward.

## Repair strategies

- strengthen competing hypotheses;
- clarify the active danger while preserving outcome uncertainty;
- seed evidence that supports both surface and hidden explanations;
- change the consequence rather than merely hiding information;
- vary timing, cost or agent when the event itself is predictable;
- show characters making decisions from incomplete models.

## Tradeoffs

- More setup improves fairness but may reduce surprise.
- More ambiguity increases possibility but can weaken investment.
- Strong genre signalling creates anticipation but narrows plausible outcomes.
- Aggressive misdirection can make characters or narration feel dishonest.
