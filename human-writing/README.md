# Human Writing Doctrine

This library governs whether generated prose feels observed, embodied, selective, and authored rather than statistically assembled.

It does not instruct a model to imitate errors, slang, or randomness. Human writing is not simulated messiness. It is the consequence of perception, motive, memory, taste, omission, contradiction, and deliberate selection.

## Governing standard

A passage is not accepted merely because it is clear, grammatical, cinematic, or emotionally legible. It must feel as though a particular consciousness chose these details, this rhythm, this silence, and this degree of disclosure for a reason.

## Core prohibitions

Never confuse human writing with:

- decorative metaphors added after the fact;
- fragmented sentences used as instant intensity;
- arbitrary quirks assigned as character voice;
- vague sensory language;
- constant emotional explanation;
- dialogue in which every person is equally articulate;
- forced cleverness;
- fake ambiguity that withholds essential causality;
- random imperfection inserted to disguise generated prose.

## Operating rule

Before drafting, identify:

- whose consciousness governs selection;
- what that person notices and habitually ignores;
- what they want concealed;
- what memory, bias, profession, culture, body, fear, or desire shapes perception;
- what the scene can leave unstated;
- what one concrete detail can carry more meaning than explanation.

After drafting, run `human-writing-gate.md`. Failure is architectural, not cosmetic. Do not simply replace words. Repair the scene's perception, pressure, selection, and disclosure.
