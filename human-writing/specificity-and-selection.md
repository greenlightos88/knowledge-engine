# Specificity and Selection

## Principle

Specificity is not the quantity of detail. It is the accuracy and dramatic usefulness of what is chosen.

Generated writing often compensates for uncertainty by adding adjectives, environmental inventory, backstory, or emotional labels. Human writing selects less and means more.

## Selection hierarchy

Prefer details that reveal, in order:

1. active danger or opportunity;
2. relationship history;
3. character strategy;
4. social or cultural reality;
5. emotional contradiction;
6. atmosphere.

Atmosphere alone is rarely enough.

## Replace categories with evidence

Avoid words such as beautiful, eerie, luxurious, poor, angry, tense, ancient, chaotic, or sad when the page can provide evidence that lets the audience make the judgment.

Do not ban abstraction. Earn it through concrete behaviour and context.

## Local truth

Objects, habits, language, food, clothing, architecture, tools, and routines must belong to the specific place, period, class, profession, and culture. Do not use surface markers as decoration. Research how the detail functions in life.

## The transfer test

Ask whether a detail could be moved unchanged into another genre, city, family, or character. If so, it is probably atmospheric rather than specific.

## The compression test

Ask which single detail could replace three explanatory lines. Keep the detail; cut the explanation.

## Common failures

- brand names used as counterfeit specificity;
- exact numbers that carry no meaning;
- culturally generic texture;
- excessive physical description before action;
- symbolic objects that advertise their symbolism;
- details selected because they sound cinematic rather than because someone would perceive or use them.

## Repair method

1. Identify the claim the prose is trying to make.
2. Remove the label.
3. Find observable evidence.
4. Tie that evidence to action, relationship, or pressure.
5. verify cultural and physical accuracy.
6. retain only what changes understanding.

## Acceptance standard

Every prominent detail must either affect action, expose consciousness, establish credible reality, or accumulate toward a later payoff. Decorative specificity does not pass.
