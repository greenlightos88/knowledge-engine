# Contradiction and Emotional Truth

## Principle

Human behaviour is often inconsistent at the surface and coherent underneath. A character may love someone and punish them, fear attention and provoke it, tell the truth to conceal a larger truth, or perform calm while making reckless choices.

Contradiction is not randomness. It is pressure acting on competing needs.

## Build the contradiction

For each major character identify:

- the stated value;
- the protected need;
- the feared exposure;
- the habitual defence;
- the behaviour that betrays the stated value;
- the condition under which the defence breaks.

## Emotional evidence

Emotion should appear through strategy, timing, attention, body, choice, and displacement—not only declarations or physical shorthand.

Avoid relying on clenched jaws, held breath, wet eyes, pounding hearts, trembling hands, or swallowed words as universal emotional currency. Such responses may be valid, but must be physically and contextually specific.

## Mixed emotion

Important moments often contain more than one emotional truth. Relief may include shame. Grief may include irritation. Love may include exhaustion. Victory may expose emptiness.

Do not flatten mixed emotion into a single label.

## Common failures

- characters accurately naming their psychology on demand;
- sudden reversals without accumulated pressure;
- contradiction treated as quirky inconsistency;
- trauma used as a complete explanation of personality;
- emotion announced before behaviour makes it legible;
- every conflict resolving through confession.

## Repair method

1. Name the two needs in collision.
2. Decide which need controls public behaviour.
3. let the suppressed need leak through a choice, mistake, fixation, or disproportionate reaction.
4. remove explanatory dialogue.
5. preserve consequences; do not excuse harmful behaviour with backstory.

## Acceptance standard

A major emotional beat must be understandable without being completely explained. Behaviour should feel surprising in the moment and inevitable in retrospect.
