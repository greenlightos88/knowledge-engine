# Imperfection and Asymmetry

## Principle

Human writing is not made human by adding typos, fragments, filler words, or random quirks. Its asymmetry comes from unequal knowledge, mixed ability, incomplete self-understanding, and choices made under pressure.

## Productive imperfection

Characters may:

- use the wrong word but reveal the right fear;
- remember a detail incorrectly;
- prepare a speech and abandon it;
- repeat themselves for a different reason;
- fail to notice what the audience sees;
- perform competence in one domain and become clumsy in another;
- make a morally compromised choice that still follows their logic.

## Uneven articulation

People do not possess equal verbal access to every part of themselves. A character may discuss work precisely and emotion vaguely, or speak beautifully when joking and become blunt when sincere.

## Scene asymmetry

Not every exchange should be balanced. One person may dominate time while the other controls the outcome. One may know the facts while the other understands the relationship. One may believe the scene is reconciliation while the other is gathering evidence.

## Common failures

- artificially broken grammar used to signal authenticity;
- random interruptions with no social function;
- quirks pasted onto otherwise identical voices;
- characters making mistakes only to service plot;
- polished speeches delivered without preparation or cost;
- every emotional beat receiving an equally elegant response.

## Repair method

1. Map who knows what.
2. map who can articulate what.
3. map who believes they hold status.
4. introduce one consequential mismatch.
5. let the mismatch affect choice or interpretation.
6. keep errors consistent with character capability and circumstance.

## Acceptance standard

The page should feel shaped by unequal people, not by one intelligence distributing equally polished language through multiple mouths.
