# Voice and Idiolect

## Principle

Voice is not an accent, catchphrase, vocabulary list, or level of profanity. It is the repeated pattern by which a consciousness selects, organizes, avoids, and expresses experience.

Two characters can use the same words and still sound different because they pursue different social strategies.

## Voice model

Define each major character through:

- what they notice;
- what they refuse to name;
- sentence length under calm and pressure;
- preferred level of directness;
- metaphor source, if any;
- humour mechanism;
- social mask;
- status behaviour;
- tolerance for silence;
- relationship-specific changes;
- words they would never use;
- subjects on which fluency suddenly increases or collapses.

## Relationship changes voice

A character should not speak identically to a parent, rival, lover, subordinate, stranger, and private self. Stable identity appears through variation, not sameness.

## Dialogue strategy

Every line should have a social action: test, delay, seduce, dominate, reassure, conceal, punish, recruit, dismiss, provoke, redirect, or surrender. Information is often the material used to perform the action, not the true purpose of speaking.

## Common generated failures

- everyone is equally articulate;
- every character speaks in polished thematic sentences;
- dialect reduced to phonetic spelling;
- quips distributed evenly across the cast;
- repeated names in intimate conversation;
- characters answering the exact question asked;
- exposition disguised as personal confession;
- distinct voices that disappear during plot-heavy scenes.

## Swap test

Remove character names from a page. If most lines can be reassigned without damage, voice is insufficiently differentiated.

## Repair method

1. Identify the speaker's immediate social objective.
2. Identify what they cannot safely say.
3. choose a tactic consistent with history and status.
4. shape syntax and vocabulary through that tactic.
5. allow the other person to misread, resist, interrupt, or exploit the line.
6. cut any line whose only function is audience briefing.

## Acceptance standard

Voice must remain recognizable without relying on accent, profanity, catchphrases, or biographical references. Distinction should emerge from thought pattern and social behaviour.
